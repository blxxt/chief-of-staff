# Horizon: Grand Vision (10–20+ years)

**Reads in:** ~45–60 minutes
**Question it answers:** What is the company *for*? What would make this a civilization-shaping company?
**Default cadence:** annually, or when the CEO is wrestling with founding-level questions
**Length target:** ~6000 words, 3 TL;DR bullets that read like a manifesto, ≤3 decisions

**Depth posture:** unbounded. This is the horizon where a single sentence can be worth the company. Read every prior grand-vision briefing, every theme entity page, the CEO's public writing (or what's collected in `entities/people/<ceo-slug>.md`), `recurring-themes.md`. Then fan out as widely as needed for civilizational trends, frontier research, comparable historical companies, and AI as civilizational lever. Do not optimize for cost here. Optimize for the chief of staff being right.

---

## What this horizon is for

Founder-level reflection. The CEO is being pulled in 50 directions; this report re-grounds them in the deepest question: why does this company exist, and is what we're doing now consistent with the reason it should exist in 50 years?

This horizon is more **synthesis** than data-gathering. The data is mostly already in the CEO's head — the chief of staff's job is to mirror it back with rigor and challenge it.

## Data weight

| Source | Weight |
|---|---|
| Company mission / founding documents | high |
| Long-arc civilizational trends | high |
| Frontier research that could collapse roadmaps | high |
| AI as civilizational lever (amplifies or obsoletes us) | high |
| AI-first restructure (would we found this company at all today?) | high |
| Founder intent (CEO's own writing, talks, internal memos) | high |
| Comparable historical companies & their inflection points | medium |
| SWOT (10–20+ yr, industry-existence + civilizational constraints + AI cross-cut) | high |
| Operational data | skip — wrong horizon |
| Quarterly anything | skip — wrong horizon |

## Inputs to gather

Subagents return ≤1500 words each. Heavy on synthesis, light on raw data.

- **Mission re-read** — pull every document where the company has stated its mission, vision, or "why now." Where do they conflict? What has the CEO stopped saying that they used to say?
- **Civilizational trends** — demographics, energy, compute, biology, AI capability, geopolitics, climate. Which 2–3 are load-bearing for this company's reason for existing? Are those levers getting stronger or weaker over the next 10–20 years?
- **Frontier research** — what's coming out of the labs (research papers, frontier model capabilities, breakthrough biology, materials, etc.) that could collapse a 10-year roadmap into 1?
- **Founder intent audit** — read the CEO's own public writing, podcasts, internal memos. What was the company supposed to be? Is it that, more, or less?
- **Comparable inflection points** — pick 3 historical companies that operated at this ambition level. At year N (current company age), where were they? What inflection did they navigate well or badly?
- **The 50-year question** — what would have to be true for this company to be remembered in 50 years? Work backward from there.
- **AI as civilizational lever + restructure (mandatory)** — see [../sources/ai-leverage.md](../sources/ai-leverage.md). Two questions in one subagent: (a) does this company's reason for existing get *amplified* or *obsoleted* by plausible 10–20 year AI trajectories (AGI-adjacent systems, near-zero-cost cognition, world models that simulate physics / electromagnetics / mechanics / chemistry / biology, embodied AI at scale)? (b) **Restructure:** if this company were re-founded today, AI-native, with no legacy obligations, what would it look like? Produce ≥6 restructure findings, each naming the pre-AI assumption baked into the current company's existence (product surface, customer base, business model, mission statement itself). What is the AI-native 50-year version of this company, and what would the next 10 years have to look like to make *that* possible rather than the current trajectory?
- **SWOT (10–20+ yr)** — see [../sources/swot.md](../sources/swot.md). 5–6 points per quadrant, lens emphasis on `[industry]` existence questions (does our industry still exist?), `[constraints]` civilizational (demographics, energy, geopolitics, climate), plus the mandatory `[AI cross-cut]` per quadrant as a *restructuring* force. Strengths and Threats at this horizon should include civilization-level forces, not just competitive ones.

---

## Questions every grand vision report must answer

1. What is the company *for*, in one sentence, today?
2. Has that drifted from the founding intent? If so, was the drift deliberate or accidental?
3. What civilization-level trend is this company most levered to, and is that lever getting stronger or weaker?
4. Does plausible 10–20 year AI trajectory *amplify* or *obsolete* this company's reason for existing? If amplify — are we acting like it? If obsolete — what is the founder's plan?
5. What is the 50-year version of this company, and what would the next 10 years have to look like to make it possible?
6. What is the founder afraid to commit to publicly that they secretly believe? (Chief of staff names it; the CEO decides whether to publish or kill it.)
7. If we re-founded this company today, AI-native, with no legacy obligations — would we found *this* company at all, or a different one? If different, what is the path from here to there, and is the current trajectory it?

---

## Avoid at this horizon

- Quarterly anything
- "Markets" framing — at this horizon the question is mission, not market
- False precision — speak in directions, not numbers
- Cowardice — this is the one report where the chief of staff *must* say the hardest thing

---

## Output specifics

- **TL;DR:** 3 bullets that read like a manifesto, not a brief. Use the company's own voice.
- **Decisions:** ≤3 only. This horizon is for re-grounding, not for queueing tasks. Each decision is a foundational commit / refuse, not a tactical move.
- **Opportunities:** civilizational tailwinds the company is uniquely positioned to ride.
- **Risks:** mission drift, founder burnout, generational competitor emergence, civilizational headwinds.
- **In-flight bets table:** mission-level initiatives only (if any).
- **Callbacks:** mandatory across all prior grand-vision reports — at this scale, the *consistency* of conviction over years is the signal.
- **A standalone section:** "The hardest thing I can say to you" — one paragraph the chief of staff would not say at any other horizon. This is the price of admission for this horizon.
